The problem · 01

Stress arrives as a slow curve. Help arrives as a sack.

Heat, drought and disease pressure build over hours and days. A farmer watching that build has one decision point and one product: whatever was bought at the start of the season, applied once, at a fixed dose, with no knowledge of what the weather would later do.

A sack is a number. Stress is a curve. Fixing a scalar to a curve is where the agronomy fails, long before the biology does.

The problem · 02

The science already works. The logistics do not.

Plant biostimulants and microbial bioprotectants are one of the most active areas in crop science right now, and the mechanisms are open literature. ACC deaminase has been published since the 1990s. Nothing about the biology is proprietary.

What is proprietary is everything between the fermenter and the field.

COST

Production is centralised, so the product must travel

A living inoculant is manufactured once, in one place, then formulated, dried, chilled, shipped, warehoused and finally applied. Every stage is a place where viable count drops.

·Peat, the best-performing carrier, still gives under 6 months of shelf life
·Field-quality standard is 10⁷ viable cells per gram at application
·Transit above 40 °C can inactivate or kill the culture outright
·Gram-negative PGPR do not sporulate, so drying kills them
GATE

Registration is a moat, and it is not made of science

Only a company with global sales can amortise a registration dossier. That is the real barrier to entry, and it sits downstream of every good idea a lab produces.

EUroughly 5 to 10 years and £3–5 million per biopesticide registration
USroughly US$300–400k
·Source: Bionema industry analysis. Figures to be re-checked against 2026 data
FIT

A product cannot carry a control loop

Even when the microbe arrives alive, it arrives as a fixed dose decided months earlier. The response cannot track the stress because the two are separated by a supply chain.

·Applied once, at planting, at a dose set before the season
·Blind to whether the stress it was bought for ever arrived
·Literature notes the gap: greenhouse performance often does not reproduce in the field

The solution

Move the factory, not the product

ReLeaf is a perfusion hollow-fibre membrane bioreactor small enough and cheap enough to sit where the crop is. B. subtilis grows inside it continuously. The membrane lets the protectant out and keeps every cell in.

Because the reactor is on site, the dose can be decided on the day rather than in a previous season. The farmer stops buying an outcome and starts owning a capability.

01An individual smallholder, one unit per plot
02A regional grower or cooperative, one unit per irrigation line
03A vertical farm, racked units feeding a closed nutrient loop

EL. 1

A microbe that answers to a machine

The interface is light. An optogenetic switch converts LED intensity into transcription rate, so brightness is dose. On the machine side, an AIoT controller fuses an online weather forecast with a local soil and air sensor and predicts stress before the plant shows it.

Prediction, not reaction. Because expression lags by roughly 105 minutes, a controller that waits for visible damage is already too late.

readslocal sensor · soil moisture, EC, temperature
readsonline forecast · rolling weather window
predicts1 · extreme heat event
predicts2 · drought onset
predicts3 · conditions a target pathogen prefers
openprediction model not yet validated against a field season
EL. 2

A bioreactor you can build yourself

Think printer, not fermenter. The reactor is assembled from parts a workshop can source, and the medium is a consumable you refill. We publish the bill of materials, the CAD and the firmware so a unit can be rebuilt without us.

Decentralised production only counts if the thing is repairable where it sits. Ownership without repairability is abandonment.

·Open BOM, CAD and firmware
·Medium supplied as a refillable consumable

EL. 3

Biosecurity is what makes field use legal

The hollow-fibre membrane is not only a separation step. It draws a regulatory line: the engineered organism never leaves the device, and what reaches the soil is protein.

That is the difference between contained use and deliberate release of a GMO, which in most jurisdictions are two different regulatory classes. The membrane is what lets a GMO-derived protectant be applied in a field at all.

inengineered B. subtilis, retained by the membrane
outthe protectant only, carried in permeate
openmembrane cutoff to be confirmed against the final module spec
opencontained-use classification to be verified with a Taiwan regulator, not asserted by us
Synthetic biology

A factory you can retune, not a product you replace

The reactor is one half. The other half is a chassis whose output can be changed without changing the hardware. We built the cloning workflow so that any gene B. subtilis can express drops into the same expression architecture.

1 chassis

engineered B. subtilis, held inside the membrane at all times

1 input

520 nm light, converted to transcription rate by the optogenetic switch

n outputs

any protectant or biostimulant the chassis can secrete, including designs that do not exist yet

0 cells out

retention is the containment claim, and it is testable

A

Tunable, because the actuator is analogue

Light intensity maps onto expression level through a graded response, so the reactor answers the question "how bad is it" rather than "is it happening". A dial, not a switch.

·Dose set by brightness, changed in software
·No new hardware needed to change the dosing curve
B

Customisable, including protectants nobody has shipped

The same device can produce an established biostimulant or a protein we designed ourselves. The reactor does not care which, so the platform outlives any one molecule.

·Known protectants, as a validated baseline
openour own designed protectant: sequence and assay still to be reported
C

Modular cloning, so the next team can use it

Standardised assembly means the expression cassette is a slot rather than a rebuild. Any gene expressible in B. subtilis goes into our design without redesigning the vector.

·Parts submitted to the Registry with full documentation
·Assembly protocol published alongside the hardware

What the device is for

Five things a sack of inoculant cannot do

01

On-site, on-demand

Made a metre from the root, on the day the forecast says it is needed. No cold chain, because nothing travels.

02

Sustainable

Solar-driven, continuous culture, no packaging and no shipped biomass. The consumable is medium, not product.

03

Precision agriculture

Dose tracks a predicted stress curve instead of a calendar. The output is graded, so it can answer how bad, not just whether.

04

Biosecurity

The organism stays inside the membrane. Only protein reaches the soil, which is what makes field use of a GMO-derived protectant tractable.

05

Self-assembled

Open BOM, CAD and firmware, so a workshop can build and repair a unit without us. Cost per unit still to be totalled.

Future goal

One device, then one irrigation line

The 2026 unit is a feasibility proof at single-plant scale, and costed per plant it would be absurd. That is the wrong denominator. The unit that makes economic sense is the irrigation line, and the owner is the cooperative.

Taiwan already organises agriculture this way. Farmers' associations, 產銷班 and shared machinery teams own equipment collectively, the way a village owns a well or a rice mill. A shared reactor is not a new social form here, it is an existing one.

Scaling means replicating the fibre module at constant wall shear and constant flux. Keep the physics at the membrane identical and let only the area grow.

What we do not claim

The limits, stated before a judge finds them

ReLeaf does not solve hunger. Hunger is mostly a failure of distribution and politics, not of yield. What ReLeaf addresses is narrower and testable: yield instability under chronic abiotic stress, on farms too small for a centralised input market to reach.

01

We have not measured our own transfer function. The parameters we designed against are published values, from a different organism at a different wavelength.

02

Cells do not grow inside the fibre lumen. Oxygen, feed, shear and temperature are all still candidates. We have no root cause.

03

The second containment layer does not exist. The membrane retains cells; that is one net. We were told in July to build a second and we have not.

04

Delivery stops at a reservoir. The permeate pump is unspecified and the root-zone interface is deferred.

05

Latency is hours, not minutes. About 105 minutes to half-maximum, so this is a device for creeping stress, not for a shock.

06

The stress prediction is unvalidated. No field season has tested whether the forecast plus sensor fusion actually calls heat, drought or pathogen conditions correctly.
