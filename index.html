Micro-factories for micro-farmers · Democratizing bio-production

21 July 2026

The problem is not the science

Today you cannot make a protein. You can only buy one.

Making a useful protein takes three things: a fermenter, a cold chain, and a registration dossier. None of them is a scientific problem. All three are owned by a small number of companies, and none of them can be paid off across half a hectare.

So the result is not that smallholders get served badly. It is that they are structurally not served at all. Nobody has to behave badly for this to happen. It is arithmetic.

The mechanisms themselves are open. ACC deaminase has been in the literature since the 1990s. What is proprietary is everything between the fermenter and the field.

The handover

So we stopped designing a product and started designing a factory small enough to give away.

A reactor assembled from parts a workshop can source is a reactor that can be repaired where it stands. That matters more than it sounds. Decentralised production is worthless if the thing breaks and the owner has to wait for a company to come and fix it. Ownership without repairability is just a slower kind of dependence.

So the bill of materials, the CAD and the firmware are published. A unit can be rebuilt without us, which is the only version of this that survives us graduating.

Inside, a hollow-fibre membrane does the work that would otherwise require a downstream purification plant. Engineered B. subtilis grows in the chamber and never leaves it. What crosses the membrane is protein.

That boundary is also a legal one. The organism stays contained, so what reaches the soil is a molecule rather than a living modified organism, which in most jurisdictions is an entirely different regulatory class.

Membrane cutoff is still unresolved in our own notes, and the contained-use classification has not been confirmed by a Taiwan regulator.

The point of a factory

You choose what it makes.

A product decides for you. A factory does not. The chassis is B. subtilis, the input is 520 nm light, and the expression cassette is a slot rather than a rebuild, so any gene the chassis can express drops into the same architecture.

Light intensity sets transcription rate, which means the dose is graded rather than switched. The reactor answers how much, not merely whether.

The machine does not care which protein it is producing. That is what makes it a platform instead of a product, and it is why this outlives any single molecule we happen to have chosen this year.

Move the factory and these stop existing

Three problems that only exist because the protein has to travel.

Shelf life

A living inoculant is formulated, dried, chilled, warehoused and only then applied, and viable count falls at every stage. Peat, the best carrier anyone has found, still gives under six months. Make the protein on the day it is used and the clock never starts.

The cold chain

Transit above 40 °C can inactivate a culture outright, and Gram-negative PGPR do not sporulate, so drying kills them too. A cold chain is an expensive answer to a question that disappears when nothing is shipped.

Distance

Every kilometre between the fermenter and the root is loss, cost and delay that the farmer pays for and does not control. Ours is a metre, and it runs on the day the forecast says the crop will need it.

Who decides what it makes

A factory is only handed over if somebody else gets to set it running.

We did not design this and then go looking for farmers to approve it. The order was the other way round, and the design still carries their fingerprints.

What we do not claim

The honest size of this.

ReLeaf does not solve hunger. Hunger is mostly a failure of distribution and politics, not of yield, and a page that implies otherwise should not be trusted on anything else it says.

What this addresses is narrower and testable: the fact that a smallholder cannot obtain a living bioprotectant on terms that make sense, and cannot make one either. Every claim above has a limit, and here they are.

Not yet shown
We have not measured our own transfer function. The parameters we designed against are published values from a different organism at a different wavelength.

Not yet shown
Cells do not grow inside the fibre lumen yet. Oxygen, feed, shear and temperature are all still candidates and we have no root cause.

Not yet shown
Containment rests on one membrane. The second layer we were told in July to build does not exist.

Not yet costed
There is no bill of materials total, so every affordability claim on this page is a hypothesis rather than a result.

Who built it

A high-school team in Taiwan.

We are not a company and we are not a lab with a budget. That turns out to matter, because a machine designed by people who had to source every part themselves ends up being a machine other people can source too.
