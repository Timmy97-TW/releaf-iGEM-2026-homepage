Problem · 01

Stress arrives as a slow curve.
Help arrives as a sack.

Heat, drought and disease pressure build across hours and days. A farmer watching that build has one decision point and one product: whatever was bought at the start of the season, applied once, at a dose fixed before anyone knew what the weather would do.

A sack is a number. Stress is a curve. Fitting a number to a curve is where the agronomy fails, long before the biology does.

Salt-affected soil
1.38 billion ha, about 10.7% of global land area. FAO, first global assessment in 50 years, 2024.
Who absorbs drought loss
83% of all economic loss from drought lands on agriculture. FAO.
Farm size
84% of the world's 570 million farms are under two hectares, and they grow about a third of the world's food. FAO · Our World in Data.

Problem · 02

The science already works.
The logistics do not.

Microbial bioprotectants are one of the most active areas in crop science, and the mechanisms are open literature. ACC deaminase has been published since the 1990s. Nothing about the biology is proprietary.

What is proprietary is everything between the fermenter and the field. A living product is manufactured once, in one place, then formulated, dried, chilled, shipped, warehoused, and only then applied. Every stage is a place where the viable count drops. Peat, the best-performing carrier anyone has found, still gives under six months. Transit above 40 °C can kill the culture outright. Gram-negative PGPR do not sporulate, so drying kills them too.

Then the dossier. Only a company with global sales can amortise a registration, which means the barrier to entry sits downstream of every good idea a lab has ever had. The moat is not made of science.

Viability against distance. The line does not arrive; it meets the floor before it gets there.

EU registration
Roughly 5 to 10 years and £3–5 million per biopesticide. Bionema industry analysis. Not re-checked against 2026 figures.
US registration
Roughly US$300–400k. Same source, same caveat.
Why this matters here
Fixed cost is the whole argument. A cold chain and a dossier cannot amortise across half a hectare, so the market does not fail smallholders out of malice. It fails them by arithmetic.
The solution

Move the factory, not the product.

A perfusion hollow-fibre membrane bioreactor, small enough and cheap enough to sit where the crop is. B. subtilis grows in it continuously. The membrane lets the protectant out and keeps every cell in. Because the reactor is on site, the dose is decided on the day rather than in a previous season.

Deployment unit
Per plot for a smallholder; per irrigation line for a cooperative; racked for a vertical farm. The unit that makes economic sense is the line, not the plant.
Cost per unit
Not yet totalled from a real bill of materials. Until it is, every affordability claim on this page is a hypothesis.
EL. 1

A microbe that answers to a machine

The interface is light. An optogenetic switch turns LED intensity into transcription rate, so brightness is dose, and the response is graded rather than switched. On the machine side, a controller fuses an online forecast with a local soil and air sensor and predicts stress before the plant shows it.

Prediction, not reaction. Expression lags by roughly 105 minutes. A controller that waits for visible damage has already lost.

EL. 2

A reactor you can open yourself

Think printer, not fermenter. The reactor is assembled from parts a workshop can source, and the medium is a consumable you refill. Bill of materials, CAD and firmware are published so a unit can be rebuilt without us.

Decentralised production only counts if the thing is repairable where it stands. Ownership without repairability is abandonment.

EL. 3

Containment is what makes field use legal

The membrane is not only a separation step. It draws a regulatory line: the engineered organism never leaves the device, and what reaches the soil is protein.

That is the difference between contained use and deliberate release of a GMO, which in most jurisdictions are different regulatory classes entirely. The membrane is what lets a GMO-derived protectant reach a field at all.

Synthetic biology

A factory you retune,
not a product you replace.

The reactor is one half. The other half is a chassis whose output can change without changing the hardware. Standardised assembly means the expression cassette is a slot rather than a rebuild, so any gene B. subtilis can express drops into the same architecture.

Designed curve, drawn from published parameters for a different organism at a different wavelength. We have not measured our own. When we do, measured points go on this axis in solid ink and this caption is deleted.

Future goal · self-audit

One device, then one irrigation line

The 2026 unit is a feasibility proof at single-plant scale, and costed per plant it would be absurd. That is the wrong denominator. The unit that makes economic sense is the irrigation line, and the owner is the cooperative.

Taiwan already organises agriculture this way. Farmers' associations, 產銷班 and shared machinery teams own equipment collectively, the way a village owns a well or a rice mill. A shared reactor is not a new social form here. It is an existing one, and 輪灌 is already the rule for taking turns.

Every claim, with its own limit

The optogenetic switch gives a graded, analogue response, so dose can track severity.

NOT YET SHOWN — we have not measured our own transfer function. The parameters we designed against are published values from a different organism at a different wavelength.

The reactor sustains a continuous culture that can be driven by light.

NOT YET SHOWN — cells do not grow inside the fibre lumen. Oxygen, feed, shear and temperature are all still candidates and we have no root cause.

The membrane contains the engineered organism, so only protein reaches the field.

NOT YET SHOWN — the second containment layer does not exist. We were told in July to build one and we have not. Membrane cutoff is also unresolved in our own notes.

The protectant is delivered to the root zone.

NOT YET SHOWN — delivery stops at a reservoir. The permeate pump is unspecified and the root-zone interface is deferred.

The controller predicts heat, drought and pathogen-favourable conditions before damage appears.

NOT YET SHOWN — no field season has tested whether forecast and sensor fusion actually call any of the three correctly.

What we do not claim
ReLeaf does not solve hunger. Hunger is mostly a failure of distribution and politics, not of yield. What this addresses is narrower and testable: yield instability under chronic abiotic stress, on farms too small for a centralised input market to reach.
Latency
About 105 minutes to half-maximum. This is a device for creeping stress, not for a shock, and the whole control strategy follows from that one number.
Scale-up rule
Replicate the fibre module at constant wall shear and constant flux. Keep the physics at the membrane identical and let only the area grow.
